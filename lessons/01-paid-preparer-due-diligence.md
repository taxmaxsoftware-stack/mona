# Lesson 01: Paid Preparer Due Diligence — EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, AOTC and Head of Household

> **Tax year(s) covered:** Tax Year (TY) 2025 returns filed in calendar year 2026. TY2026 changes are flagged **⚠ YEAR CHANGE**.
> **Sources verified as of:** 2026-10-13 (IRS.gov, Instructions for Form 8867 rev. 11/2025, Pub. 596 (2025), Schedule 8812 instructions (2025), Pub. 970 (2025), Rev. Proc. 2024-40, IRS TY2026 inflation-adjustment release)
> **Audience:** New and returning paid preparers
> **Estimated time:** 75–90 minutes

**Acronyms used in this lesson:**
- **EITC/EIC** — earned income tax credit
- **CTC** — child tax credit
- **ACTC** — additional child tax credit (the refundable part of the CTC)
- **ODC** — credit for other dependents
- **AOTC** — American opportunity tax credit
- **HOH** — head of household filing status
- **SSN** — Social Security number
- **ITIN** — individual taxpayer identification number
- **ATIN** — adoption taxpayer identification number
- **TIN** — taxpayer identification number (covers SSN, ITIN and ATIN)
- **AGI** — adjusted gross income
- **PTIN** — preparer tax identification number

## 1. Learning Objectives

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. Identify the four tax benefits covered by the paid-preparer due-diligence rules under IRC §6695(g).
2. Explain the four due-diligence requirements in Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2 and apply them to a client interview.
3. Recognize information that is **incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete**, and write reasonable follow-up questions.
4. Document inquiries and client responses **contemporaneously**, and keep the five required records for the required period.
5. Apply the **TY2025 SSN rules** for the CTC/ACTC, and spot the **TY2026 SSN rules** for the AOTC.
6. Work out the preparer's (and the employer's) penalty exposure for a return that fails due diligence.

## 2. Plain-Language Explanation

### What "due diligence" means

Every paid preparer must use reasonable care on every return. On top of that general duty, the law adds **specific, enforceable steps** when a paid preparer decides whether a client can claim any of these four benefits:

| # | Covered benefit | Code section |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Earned income tax credit (EITC) | IRC §32 |
| 2 | Child tax credit / additional child tax credit / credit for other dependents (CTC/ACTC/ODC) | IRC §24 |
| 3 | American opportunity tax credit (AOTC) | IRC §25A(a)(1) |
| 4 | Head of household (HOH) filing status | IRC §2(b) |

Doing due diligence means **knowing the law well enough to ask the right questions, not ignoring red flags, writing down what you asked and what the client said, and keeping the records.**

### The four requirements (Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2(b))

A preparer complies with the rules when they do **all four** of the following:

1. **Complete and submit Form 8867.** Fill it out truthfully and accurately, carry out the actions it describes, and submit it the required way (see §4 below).
2. **Compute the credit(s) correctly.** Complete the applicable IRS worksheet for the EITC, the CTC/ACTC/ODC and/or the AOTC, or your own worksheet that shows the same information.
3. **Meet the knowledge requirement.** Interview the client, ask adequate questions, review adequate information, and **document the questions and answers at the time you ask them**. You may not use information you know, or have reason to know, is incorrect. You may not ignore what the information implies. You **must make reasonable inquiries** when a reasonable, well-informed preparer would conclude the information looks **incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete**.
4. **Keep records for 3 years.** The five required records are listed in §4.

> **Key point:** The IRS reports that **failing the knowledge requirement** is the most common reason it assesses a due-diligence penalty.

### The penalty

IRC §6695(g) sets a **$500** base penalty for **each failure**. Under IRC §6695(h) that amount is **indexed for inflation**. Each covered benefit on a return counts as a separate failure, so one return can carry up to **four** penalties.

| Return or claim filed in calendar year | Penalty per failure | Maximum per return (4 benefits) |
|---|---|---|
| 2025 | $635 | $2,540 |
| 2026 | $650 | $2,600 |

**⚠ YEAR CHANGE:** The penalty amount changes every year. It is tied to the year the return is **filed**, not the tax year. Check IRS.gov/Form8867 for the current figure.

**The employer can be penalized too.** Under Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2(c), the IRS can assess the penalty against a **firm** for an employee's failure, for example when the firm's management took part in or knew about the failure, or when the firm had no reasonable compliance procedures (or did not follow them).

**Consequences for the client** (separate from the preparer penalty): the credit can be disallowed, and accuracy or fraud penalties can apply. The client may also have to file **Form 8862** before claiming the credit again, and can be **banned from claiming the credit for 2 years** (reckless or intentional disregard of the rules) or **10 years** (fraud).

### Year-sensitive rules you must know for the 2026 filing season and beyond

**⚠ YEAR CHANGE — CTC/ACTC (starting TY2025), P.L. 119-21 (One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act):**
- The maximum **CTC rose to $2,200** per qualifying child for TY2025. Up to **$1,700** of it is refundable as the ACTC.
- **New for TY2025:** the **taxpayer** must have a valid SSN to claim the CTC/ACTC. On a joint return, **only one spouse** needs a valid SSN. The other spouse may have an SSN or ITIN issued on or before the due date of the return (including extensions).
- **Continuing rule:** each **qualifying child** must have an SSN that is valid for employment and issued before the due date of the return (including extensions). A child with only an ITIN or ATIN cannot be claimed for the CTC/ACTC, but may qualify the client for the **$500 ODC**.
- The law also indexes the CTC for inflation starting with TY2026. Confirm the TY2026 amounts in **Rev. Proc. 2025-32** and the TY2026 Schedule 8812 instructions before using them.

**⚠ YEAR CHANGE — AOTC (starting TY2026):**
- For **TY2025**, the taxpayer, the spouse (if filing jointly) and the student must each have a valid **TIN** (SSN, ITIN or ATIN) by the due date of the return, including extensions.
- **Beginning in TY2026:** the **taxpayer, or the spouse on a joint return, and the student (if a dependent)** must have an **SSN valid for work**, issued before the due date of the 2026 return (including extensions). An ITIN will no longer qualify.

**EITC — indexed annually:**

| EITC figure | TY2025 | TY2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Maximum credit, 3+ qualifying children | $8,046 | $8,231 |
| Maximum credit, no qualifying child | $649 | See Rev. Proc. 2025-32 |
| Investment income limit | $11,950 | See Rev. Proc. 2025-32 |
| AGI limit, 3+ children (single/HOH/MFS/QSS ÷ MFJ) | $61,555 ÷ $68,675 | See Rev. Proc. 2025-32 |

Sources: Rev. Proc. 2024-40 and Pub. 596 (2025) for TY2025. IRS TY2026 inflation-adjustment news release for the $8,231 figure.

**EITC and children without a valid SSN.** If the client has a qualifying child, follow the **with-qualifying-child** rules to decide whether the client is eligible, even if none of the children has a valid SSN. When **figuring the amount** of the credit, count **only** the children who have valid SSNs.

## 3. Applicable IRS Forms

| Form / Schedule | Role in due diligence |
|---|---|
| **Form 8867**, Paid Preparer's Due Diligence Checklist | Required with every return or amended return on which a paid preparer determined eligibility for the EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, AOTC and/or HOH. E-file accepts up to four Forms 8867 per return. |
| **Form 1040 / 1040-SR / 1040-NR / 1040-SS** | The return the benefit is claimed on. Form 8867 must relate to the same tax year. |
| **Schedule EIC** (Form 1040) | Gives qualifying-child information for the EITC. |
| **Schedule 8812** (Form 1040) | Figures the CTC, ACTC and ODC. Its instructions contain the computation worksheets. |
| **Form 8863** | Figures education credits. Its instructions contain the AOTC worksheet. |
| **Form 1098-T** | Tuition statement from the school. Generally required to claim the AOTC, but it may **not** match the amounts actually paid. |
| **Form 8332** | Custodial parent's release of a claim to exemption for a child. Allows the **noncustodial** parent to claim the CTC/ACTC/ODC. It does **not** transfer the EITC or HOH. Check whether the release has been revoked. |
| **Form 8862** | Information to claim certain credits after a disallowance. Required (unless an exception applies) when the EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC or AOTC was denied in a prior year for a reason other than a math or clerical error. |
| **Schedule C** (Form 1040) | Self-employment income and expenses. These determine earned income for the EITC and ACTC. |
| **EITC worksheets** (Form 1040 instructions) | The computation worksheets that satisfy requirement #2. |

## 4. Due-Diligence Requirements

### 4.1 Requirement checklist

| Requirement | What you must do | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| **Form 8867** | Complete it truthfully and accurately, based on information you got from the client. Carry out the actions it describes. Submit it the required way. | Reg. §1.6695-2(b)(1) |
| **Computation** | Complete the IRS worksheet (or your own equivalent) for each credit claimed. | Reg. §1.6695-2(b)(2) |
| **Knowledge** | Know the law. Ask adequate questions. Make reasonable inquiries when information looks incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete. **Document the inquiries and responses contemporaneously** (at the time). | Reg. §1.6695-2(b)(3) |
| **Retention** | Keep the five records below for 3 years. | Reg. §1.6695-2(b)(4) |

### 4.2 How to submit Form 8867

| Your role | What you do with Form 8867 |
|---|---|
| Signing preparer, **e-filing** the return | E-file Form 8867 with the return. |
| Signing preparer, **not** e-filing and not mailing the return | Give Form 8867 to the client with instructions to file it with the return. |
| Signing preparer **mailing** the return for the client (only after the client has reviewed and signed it) | Mail Form 8867 with the return. |
| **Nonsigning** preparer who determined eligibility for a covered benefit | Give your completed Form 8867 (paper or electronic) to the signing preparer. |

When more than one preparer determines eligibility for different benefits on the same return, **each one completes their own Form 8867.**

### 4.3 The five records to keep

1. A copy of **Form 8867**.
2. The **completed worksheet(s)** for each credit claimed.
3. Copies of **documents the client gave you** that you relied on.
4. A record of **how, when and from whom** you got the information used for Form 8867 and the worksheets.
5. A record of **any other information you relied on**, including **the questions you asked and the client's answers**.

**Retention period:** 3 years from the **latest** of:
- the due date of the return (**not** including extensions);
- the date the return was filed (signing preparer who e-files);
- the date the return was given to the client for signature (signing preparer who does not e-file); or
- the date a nonsigning preparer gave their part of the return to the signing preparer.

Records may be paper or electronic. Electronic records must meet Rev. Proc. 97-22.

### 4.4 Documents you may rely on (examples, not required items)

| Issue | Example documents |
|---|---|
| **Child's residency** | School records, landlord or property-management statement, health-care provider statement, medical records, child-care provider records, placement-agency statement, social-service records, place-of-worship statement, Indian tribal official statement |
| **Child's disability** | Statement from a doctor, another health-care provider, or a social-services agency or program |
| **Schedule C** | Business license, Forms 1099, records of gross receipts and expenses, the client's own income and expense summaries, bank statements |

> If you already asked for and reviewed documents for the same tax year, you don't need to ask again. **Prior-year knowledge can count too.** If last year you verified that the children were legally adopted, you don't have to re-ask about the relationship this year. You must still ask about anything that **changes from year to year**, such as how long the children lived with the client and what the client's income was.

### 4.5 Benefit-specific questions to ask (beyond the Form 8867 prompts)

Form 8867 asks only a few targeted questions for each benefit. **The client must meet *all* the eligibility rules**, even if every Form 8867 answer is "Yes."

- **EITC:** Is there a qualifying child (relationship, age, residency for more than half the year, joint-return test)? Could the child be someone else's qualifying child too, so that the **tiebreaker rules** apply? Is investment income within the limit for the tax year? Is the reported earned income complete and believable, especially Schedule C income?
- **CTC/ACTC/ODC:** Does each child have an SSN valid for employment, issued by the due date? (**TY2025+**) Does the taxpayer, or one spouse on a joint return, have a valid SSN? Is the child under 17 at year-end? If the parents are divorced or separated, who is the custodial parent? Is there a Form 8332, and has it been revoked?
- **AOTC:** Was the student enrolled at least half time in a degree or credential program? How many prior years has the AOTC (or Hope credit) been claimed? What qualified expenses were **actually paid** during the calendar year, and by whom? Was a Form 1098-T received? Are the TIN or SSN requirements met for **this** tax year?
- **HOH:** Was the client unmarried, or **considered unmarried**, on the last day of the year? Did the client pay **more than half the cost of keeping up the home** for the year? Did a qualifying person live with the client for more than half the year (with exceptions, such as a dependent parent)?

### 4.6 Penalty exception

Under Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2(d), a penalty does not apply when the preparer can show that, considering all the facts and circumstances, their normal office procedures were reasonably designed and routinely followed to ensure compliance, and the failure was isolated. **This is a narrow exception. It does not replace doing the work.**

## 5. Examples

### Example 1 — Inconsistent age and Schedule C income (based on the Form 8867 instructions)

**Facts:** A new client, age 25 and never married, wants to claim two children, ages 10 and 12. The client reports $12,000 from a lawn-care business and **no** business expenses.

**Analysis:** The client may qualify for the EITC and the ACTC. However, a 25-year-old with a 12-year-old is unusual enough to call for inquiry, and running a lawn-care business with **zero** expenses is inconsistent. Reasonable inquiries include:
- Are these your foster or adopted children? If so, were they placed with you by an authorized placement agency or court order, or lawfully placed for adoption?
- How long did the children live with you in 2025?
- If you aren't the parent, did another relative also live with the children for more than half of 2025?
- How much do you charge per lawn, and how many lawns did you service?
- Do you have records of the money you received?
- How did you provide the service without equipment, fuel or supplies?

**Result:** Ask the questions and **write down the questions and answers at the time you ask them**. Use the answers to decide whether the client is eligible and what their earned income is.

### Example 2 — A returning client

**Facts:** Same client as Example 1, but you also prepared their 2024 return and verified then that the children were legally adopted.

**Result:** You don't need to re-ask about the **relationship** in order to meet the knowledge requirement for the 2025 return. You must still confirm the facts that change from year to year: residency for 2025, 2025 income and 2025 expenses.

### Example 3 — Two preparers, two Forms 8867

**Facts:** Preparer A determines EITC eligibility. Preparer B, who works in the same office, determines AOTC eligibility and signs the return.

**Result:** Two Forms 8867 are required. Preparer A (nonsigning) completes one for the EITC and gives it to Preparer B. Preparer B completes one for the AOTC and files both with the return.

### Example 4 — The Form 1098-T is not proof of payment

**Facts:** A client's Form 1098-T shows qualified tuition and related expenses. The client mentions that part of the bill is still unpaid, and a grant covered some of it.

**Result:** The AOTC is based on qualified expenses **actually paid** during the calendar year. Form 1098-T amounts may not match the amounts actually paid. Ask for the student account statement or payment records, account for scholarships and grants, and document how you arrived at the expense figure.

### Example 5 — ⚠ YEAR CHANGE: CTC with a mixed-TIN family (TY2025)

**Facts:** A married couple filing jointly for TY2025. Spouse 1 has an SSN valid for employment. Spouse 2 has an ITIN issued before the due date. Their child, age 6, has an SSN valid for employment, issued before the due date.

**Result:** The couple can claim the CTC/ACTC for the child, provided all other requirements are met. Under the TY2025 rules, only **one** spouse on a joint return needs a valid SSN. If **neither** spouse had a valid SSN, no CTC/ACTC would be allowed. The preparer would then consider whether the couple qualifies for the ODC.

## 6. Common Preparer Mistakes

| Mistake | Why it's wrong | How to avoid it |
|---|---|---|
| Treating Form 8867 as a box-ticking exercise | Answering "Yes" on Form 8867 does not establish eligibility. **All** eligibility rules must be met. | Use Form 8867 as a minimum. Ask questions about every eligibility rule. |
| Not documenting questions and answers at the time | The knowledge requirement calls for **contemporaneous** documentation. Notes written after the fact (e.g., during an exam) are weak evidence. | Put a notes field in the intake workflow and record the question, the answer and the date during the interview. |
| Accepting round-number or no-expense Schedule C income | Overstated or made-up self-employment income can inflate the EITC and ACTC. | Ask about customers, pricing, records, equipment and expenses, and ask for supporting records. |
| Using the Form 1098-T figure as the AOTC expense | Form 1098-T may not show amounts actually paid in the year. | Verify payments and account for scholarships and grants. |
| Claiming the CTC for a child with an ITIN | The child must have an SSN valid for employment, issued by the due date. | Check each child's TIN. Consider the ODC instead. |
| Applying pre-2025 CTC rules to the taxpayer's own TIN (**⚠ YEAR CHANGE**) | Starting in TY2025, the taxpayer (or one spouse on a joint return) must have a valid SSN. | Update your intake questions every year. Check the "What's New" section of the Form 8867 and Schedule 8812 instructions. |
| Carrying TY2025 AOTC TIN rules into TY2026 (**⚠ YEAR CHANGE**) | Starting in TY2026, an ITIN no longer qualifies the taxpayer or a dependent student for the AOTC. | Check the year-specific rules in Pub. 970 and the Form 8863 instructions. |
| Letting a noncustodial parent with Form 8332 claim the EITC or HOH | Form 8332 releases the dependency claim (and with it the CTC/ODC), **not** the EITC or HOH. | Ask who the custodial parent is. Read Form 8332 and check whether it has been revoked. |
| Accepting HOH without asking about household costs | HOH requires paying **more than half** the cost of keeping up the home. | Ask about rent or mortgage, utilities, food eaten in the home, and who else contributes. |
| Skipping Form 8862 after a prior disallowance | The credit will be denied unless Form 8862 is attached, when it is required. | Ask every client whether any credit was denied in a prior year, and why. |
| Using last year's penalty, threshold or credit amounts | Many amounts are indexed every year. | Use the year-sensitive figures table, then verify against the current Rev. Proc. and instructions. |

## 7. Practice Scenario

**Use the demo environment to work through this scenario.**

**Tax year:** 2025 (return prepared in February 2026). **You are the signing preparer and you will e-file.**

**Client:** Jordan Rivera (fictional), age 29, never married. SSN valid for employment.

**Intake facts, as the client gave them:**
1. Jordan wants to file as **head of household**.
2. Two children live with Jordan:
   - **Leo**, age 8. Jordan's son. SSN valid for employment.
   - **Mia**, age 4. Jordan's daughter, born abroad. She has an **ITIN**. Her SSN application is "in process."
3. Jordan, Leo and Mia live in an apartment **leased in the name of Jordan's mother**, who also lives there. Jordan says, "My mom handles the rent; I buy groceries sometimes."
4. Jordan ran a house-cleaning business. Jordan reports **$14,000** in gross receipts, all cash, and **no expenses**.
5. Jordan says the EITC was "taken away" two years ago after an IRS letter, but doesn't remember why.
6. Jordan has no other income and no investment income.

**Tasks:**
1. List each covered benefit that could apply, and the Form 8867 parts you'll complete.
2. For **HOH**, identify the eligibility problem in the facts. Write at least **three** reasonable inquiries.
3. For **Mia**, explain which child-related benefits she can and cannot support for TY2025, and why.
4. For **Schedule C**, write at least **four** reasonable inquiries, and list the documents you'd ask for.
5. For **item 5**, explain what you need to find out and which form might be needed.
6. Describe exactly what you'll **document** and how long you must **keep** it.

### Model answer (instructor key)

1. **Benefits:** the EITC, the CTC/ACTC/ODC and HOH. AOTC: not applicable. You'll complete Part I and the EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC and HOH sections of Form 8867.
2. **HOH:** Jordan's mother holds the lease and "handles the rent," which raises doubt that Jordan paid **more than half the cost of keeping up the home**. Inquiries:
   - What was the total yearly rent, and who paid it?
   - Who paid the utilities?
   - What did the household spend on food eaten at home, and who paid for it?
   - Does your mother claim either child, or claim HOH?
   - Do you have any records of the payments you made?

   If Jordan didn't pay more than half, **HOH is not allowed**. Jordan would file as single. Also note that the mother may have her own claim to the children, which can bring in the **tiebreaker rules**. As parent, Jordan generally wins the tiebreaker if Jordan claims the children. The mother could claim a child only if her AGI is higher than Jordan's **and** Jordan does not claim that child.
3. **Mia (ITIN):**
   - **CTC/ACTC: not allowed.** For TY2025, a child claimed for the CTC/ACTC must have an SSN valid for employment, issued by the due date of the return (including extensions). If Mia's SSN is issued **by** that due date, the CTC can be reconsidered. Otherwise, she cannot qualify Jordan for the CTC on an original **or** amended 2025 return.
   - **ODC: may be allowed** if Mia is Jordan's dependent and has a TIN issued by the due date.
   - **EITC:** Mia still counts as a qualifying child when deciding **whether** Jordan qualifies under the with-child rules. She does **not** count toward the **amount** of the credit. The credit is figured with **one** qualifying child (Leo), assuming Leo meets all the tests.
   - **Jordan's own SSN** meets the new **⚠ TY2025** taxpayer-SSN requirement for the CTC claimed for Leo.
4. **Schedule C inquiries:**
   - How many clients did you have, and what did you charge per visit?
   - How did you get paid, and do you have any deposit records?
   - Did you buy cleaning supplies or equipment, or have vehicle or mileage costs?
   - Do you have a business license, appointment book, invoices or a receipts log?
   - Did any client issue you a Form 1099-NEC or 1099-K?

   Documents to request: gross-receipts records, bank statements, expense receipts, any Forms 1099, business license. If Jordan cannot back up the income reasonably, **you must not use information you know or have reason to know is incorrect**.
5. **Prior disallowance:** Find out whether the EITC was denied for a reason **other than a math or clerical error**, and whether a ban period applied. Ask Jordan for the IRS notice. Unless an exception applies, **Form 8862** must be attached to claim the EITC again, and the credit cannot be claimed during any ban period.
6. **Documentation and retention:** Keep Form 8867, the EITC and Schedule 8812 worksheets, copies of the documents relied on, a record of how, when and from whom the information was obtained, and the questions and answers recorded **during** the interview. Keep them for **3 years** from the **later** of the return's due date (April 15, 2026, not counting extensions) or the e-file date.

**Q8.** An employee of a tax firm repeatedly skips the knowledge requirement. The firm has **no written due-diligence procedures**, and its manager knew about the shortcuts. Who can be penalized?
- A. Only the employee.

**Q10.** ⚠ *Year-sensitive.* A single parent with an **ITIN** pays tuition for their dependent child, who has an SSN valid for work, at an eligible college. Which statement is correct?
- A. The parent can claim the AOTC for both TY2025 and TY2026, because the student has an SSN.

## 9. Answer Explanations

**Q1 — C.** Under the knowledge requirement, a preparer must make reasonable inquiries when information looks incorrect, inconsistent or incomplete, and must document those inquiries at the time (Reg. §1.6695-2(b)(3); Form 8867 instructions, Example 1).
- *A is wrong:* the duty reaches beyond the Form 8867 questions. The client must meet **all** eligibility rules.
- *B is wrong:* the facts call for questions, not refusal.
- *D is wrong:* birth certificates may establish relationship, but not residency for more than half the year, placement status, or whether someone else could claim the child.

**Q2 — D ($2,600).** For returns **filed in 2026**, the penalty is $650 per failure, and each covered benefit is a separate failure: $650 × 4 = $2,600.
- *A* is the unindexed statutory base.
- *B* applies the $500 base to all four benefits.
- *C* uses the **2025-filing** amount ($635 × 4). **⚠ The penalty is tied to the year the return is filed.**

**Q3 — B (April 15, 2029).** Records are kept for 3 years from the **latest** of the due date (**not** counting extensions) or, for an e-filing signing preparer, the date filed. April 15, 2026 is later than March 3, 2026, so the period runs to April 15, 2029.
- *A* uses the earlier date.
- *C* wrongly counts an extension.
- *D* has no basis in the regulation.

**Q4 — B.** Starting in TY2025 (P.L. 119-21), the taxpayer must have a valid SSN to claim the CTC/ACTC. On a joint return, only one filer needs one, and the other may have an ITIN issued by the due date (Schedule 8812 instructions (2025); Form 8867 instructions, What's New). The child's SSN requirement is also met.
- *A* misstates the joint-return rule.
- *C* and *D* are wrong because the requirements for the CTC are met.

**Q5 — C.** A child must have an SSN valid for employment, issued by the due date, to be claimed for the CTC/ACTC. A child with an ITIN or ATIN may still qualify the client for the ODC (Form 8867 instructions, Reminders).
- *A* and *B* are wrong: both the CTC and ACTC require the child's SSN.
- *D* is wrong because the ODC may be available.

**Q6 — B.** The AOTC is based on qualified expenses **actually paid** in the calendar year. Form 1098-T may not reflect amounts actually paid, so the preparer must verify (Form 8867 instructions, Part IV; Pub. 970).
- *A* is wrong: Form 1098-T is a statement from the school, and it isn't proof of payment.
- *C* is wrong: switching credits doesn't remove the need to verify expenses.
- *D* is wrong: capping the expense at $4,000 skips the verification and the grant adjustment.

**Q7 — B.** A custodial parent's release (Form 8332 or an equivalent) lets the noncustodial parent claim the child as a dependent for the CTC/ACTC/ODC. It does **not** transfer the EITC or HOH (Pub. 501; Pub. 596). The preparer should also ask whether there is a later revocation (Form 8867 instructions, Part III).
- *A* overstates what Form 8332 does.
- *C* is backwards.
- *D* ignores the release rules.

**Q8 — C.** The preparer is liable for their own failures, and the firm can also be penalized when, for example, management took part in or knew of the failure, or when the firm failed to set up or follow reasonable compliance procedures (Treas. Reg. §1.6695-2(c)).
- *A* and *B* each leave out one of the two parties.
- *D* is wrong: the client's signature does not shift the preparer's duty.

**Q9 — B.** When the EITC (or the CTC/ACTC/ODC, or the AOTC) was denied for a reason other than a math or clerical error, Form 8862 is required to claim it again, unless an exception applies (Form 8867 instructions; Form 8862 instructions). Current-year due diligence still applies.
- *A* skips a required form.
- *C* has no basis.
- *D* applies the fraud ban, which the facts don't describe.

**Q10 — B.** **⚠ YEAR CHANGE.** For TY2025, the taxpayer and student need a valid TIN (an SSN, ITIN or ATIN) by the due date. **Beginning in TY2026**, the taxpayer (or the spouse on a joint return) and the dependent student must have an SSN valid for work, issued before the due date (IRS Education Credits page; Pub. 970 (2025); Pub. 4772).
- *A* ignores the 2026 change.
- *C* ignores the TY2025 rule.
- *D* is wrong: Form 8862 deals with prior disallowances, not TIN eligibility.

**Q11 — Sample answer.** Inquiries:
1. "Which platforms did you deliver for, and did you receive a Form 1099-K or 1099-NEC, or an earnings statement?"
2. "What vehicle did you use, and how many business miles did you drive? Do you have a mileage log or a platform mileage report?"
3. "What other costs did you have, such as phone, insulated bags or parking?"

You could also ask how the $18,000 figure was calculated. Documentation: record each question, the client's answer, the date, and any documents reviewed (e.g., "Reviewed platform annual summary showing $18,000 gross; client has no mileage log; platform app reports 9,400 miles"). Keep the records for 3 years. *Why it matters:* reporting income with no expenses is the kind of inconsistent or incomplete information that triggers the duty to make reasonable inquiries, and Schedule C income directly affects the EITC and ACTC (Form 8867 instructions, line 8 discussion; EITC Central guidance on self-employed taxpayers).

**Q12 — Sample answer.** Form 8867 asks only a few targeted questions for each benefit. The instructions say a client may not claim the EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, AOTC or HOH unless **all** the eligibility requirements are met, "even if you answer 'Yes'" to the form's questions. The preparer still has to know the full eligibility rules and ask the questions they call for.

## Key Takeaways & Closing Remarks

- Due diligence covers **four** benefits: the **EITC, CTC/ACTC/ODC, AOTC and HOH**. Each one is a separate penalty exposure.
- There are **four** requirements: **Form 8867, computation worksheets, knowledge, and record retention**.
- The knowledge requirement is where most penalties come from: **ask, evaluate, and document at the time**.
- The penalty is indexed and tied to the year the return is **filed**: **$635** (filed in 2025) and **$650** (filed in 2026), up to **$2,600** per return.
- **⚠ TY2025:** the CTC is $2,200 per child (up to $1,700 refundable), and the **taxpayer**, or one spouse on a joint return, needs a valid SSN.
- **⚠ TY2026:** the AOTC requires an SSN valid for work for the taxpayer (or the spouse on a joint return) and a dependent student. An ITIN no longer qualifies.
- Keep the **five records** for **3 years** from the latest applicable date.
